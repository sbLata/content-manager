The watering guide

Why watering is tricky

Watering is something every plant owner thinks about and there are a lot of different opinions about it, which can make it confusing, because some people say you should water on a schedule and other people say you should never water on a schedule, and the truth is that it depends on the plant, the pot, the light, the season and the temperature of your home, so a plant in a sunny window in summer will drink far more than the same plant in a dim corner in winter, and clay pots dry out faster than plastic ones, and plants with thick leaves usually store water and need less of it, while plants with thin leaves often wilt when they are thirsty, and you should also think about the water itself, since some plants dislike tap water that is very hard or heavily treated, and letting water stand overnight can help, and it is usually better to water deeply and less often than to give a little splash every day, because shallow watering encourages shallow roots, and finally you should always check the soil with your finger before watering rather than relying on the calendar alone.

Snake plants

Water snake plants once a week so the soil never dries out completely.

Drainage

Good drainage matters more than most people think. Always choose a pot with a hole in the bottom, empty the saucer about thirty minutes after watering, and never let the roots sit in standing water, because soggy soil is the most common cause of root rot in indoor plants.
